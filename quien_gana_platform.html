Quién Gana is a public-accountability platform that traces the colonial circuits moving wealth out of Puerto Rico — through pension raids, tax havens, disaster contracts, pharmaceutical extraction, energy privatization, real-estate displacement, and the federal debt machinery itself — and connects the people on every end of the wire.

The thesis

Not scandals. A machine.

Each story you've read about Puerto Rico — the debt crisis, LUMA, the crypto bros, Maria's missing billions, the privatized hospitals, the closed schools — is usually told as its own scandal. As if the actors were unrelated. As if the pattern were coincidence.

It isn't. It's one machine with many circuits. Federal law builds the channels. Wall Street and corporate America run product through them. The colony pays. The metropole collects. The same firms appear in case after case. The same Congressional moves enable each one.

Quién Gana documents the machine in public — circuit by circuit, name by name, dollar by dollar. We don't just expose extraction. We connect the wronged to the unwitting to the responsible, so that organizing becomes possible.

“La colonia paga. Wall Street cobra. El Congreso escribe las reglas para ambos.”

The infrastructure

The platform connects the investigations.

Each circuit feeds the same infrastructure: a unified map of actors, a wire that surfaces every news mention, tools for divestment campaigns, and an open contribution layer.

El Mapa

The Map · geographic view

Every actor, on the map. Act 60 settlers pinned to their addresses. Disaster contractors at their project sites. Pharma plants in their towns. Pension funds in their state capitals. Geography turns abstraction into territory.

El Cable

The Wire · live news layer

A real-time feed that surfaces every news article mentioning any entity in our databases. Quién Gana becomes the place to track ongoing fights — not a campaign that goes stale, but a living archive that organizers, journalists, and researchers can return to weekly.

Las Herramientas

The Tools · divestment toolkit

Once you learn your pension is in Oaktree, you need a next step. Pre-written letters to fund trustees. Model resolutions for union locals. A tracker of which funds have divested under pressure. The piece most accountability projects skip — and the one that turns inbound traffic into actual power.

Completar el Expediente

Complete the Record · open contribution

For every entry in every database, anyone can add documentation: photos, news clippings, court filings, personal testimony. Moderated, not free-for-all. Wiki-style depth without surrendering quality. The corpus grows without our team doing every keystroke.

Who we are

Boricuas Antifascistas.

We are diaspora and on-island Puerto Ricans building public-accountability infrastructure in service of decolonization. We are not a 501(c)3. We are not a think tank. We are not waiting for permission.

Quién Gana exists because the colony's extraction machinery is documented in scattered fragments — court filings here, SEC disclosures there, Hacienda registries somewhere else — but the fragments don't talk to each other. The connections only come into focus when someone insists on putting them together. We're insisting.
